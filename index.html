Detection methodology

Explainable statistical screening

AquaGuard compares each reading with a trailing rolling median for the same meter. Robust variability is estimated using median absolute deviation (MAD).

Screening ruleObserved > Baseline + k × Scale

A minimum excess threshold is also applied. Consecutive flags are grouped into candidate events to reduce alert noise.

An alert indicates an unusual pattern. It does not prove a leak, identify its physical cause or replace on-site inspection.

How the forecast works

For each future day, AquaGuard blends the median of up to four recent observations for the same weekday (70%) with the median of the most recent 14 daily totals (30%). The method is intentionally transparent and suitable as a baseline for limited history.

Weather, occupancy, holidays, academic calendars and operational changes are not modelled. This forecast is a prototype estimate, not a guarantee of future demand.

Project overview

Smarter water management starts with insight.

AquaGuard AI · Explainable water anomaly detection and demand planning for university campuses.

University Track
The challenge

Water use is easy to measure.
Changes are harder to notice.

Universities use water across classrooms, laboratories, washrooms, kitchens, residences and landscaping. Periodic readings and monthly bills may reveal unusual consumption only after it has happened. Hidden leaks and inefficient patterns can continue without timely insight.

⌁

Monitor & detect

Upload interval readings through CSV. The dashboard organizes consumption and screens for unusual increases using a meter-specific recent baseline.

◈

Explain & forecast

Alerts include the observed volume, expected baseline and estimated excess. A weekday-aware model forecasts demand for the next seven days.

◎

Simulate & improve

Explore hypothetical response delays and conservation scenarios to support informed operational planning.

Prototype scope

The demonstration uses a reproducible synthetic dataset representing five virtual water meters, 28 days of history and 15-minute measurement intervals — 13,440 readings in total. No physical sensors or real campus infrastructure are connected.

The prototype demonstrates a software workflow. It does not claim field validation, measured savings or confirmed leak diagnoses.

Expected impact

01
Earlier investigation

Help facilities teams notice unusual patterns and prioritize inspections.

02
Better planning

Use short-term demand outlooks to support allocation discussions.

03
Accessible starting point

Begin with existing CSV meter exports before purchasing monitoring hardware.

Future development

The next milestone is an authorized university pilot: connect real campus meters, validate units and intervals, confirm candidate events through inspection, calibrate thresholds, measure false alarms and track actual water-saving outcomes. Production deployment would also require authentication, audit logs, secure storage and operational integration.
